Episode 14: Drip, Drip, Horray!

November 21, 2025 · 17 min

Ep. 14 — Drip, Drip, Horray!

Chapters

Summary

It’s Tuesday in Maple Grove, which can only mean one thing: the blood bus has arrived, bringing with it free coffee, dusty cookies, and enough medical confusion to qualify as an Olympic sport. John (Dusty) shows up buzzing with newfound confidence after “unlocking” a diagnostic talent he calls Clue-dini — a skill that terrifies Fred (Farts), irritates Hope, and confuses everyone else.

Inside the bus, Dr. Bobby is running at three espressos and one questionable muffin, proudly unveiling his latest scientific initiative: The Dough Division, a team of basement hobbyists creating experimental rye bread so dense it briefly lowers IQ.

Gigi arrives to deliver the R-word of the day.

Hope narrates the spiral as the Old Farts Corner becomes ground zero for Clue-dini guesses, candy-based emergencies, rye-bread philosophy, and the kind of small-town nonsense that makes Tuesday the most dangerous day of the week.

Voices You’ll Hear

  • John (Dusty) – self-appointed medical mystic with a brand-new overinflated ego
  • Fred (Farts) – cookie victim, skeptic of all diagnostics, and Old Farts Corner loyalist
  • Hope – narrator, composed observer, and keeper of Maple Grove sanity
  • Dr. Bobby – espresso-powered physician, rye-bread innovator, and peppermint-breath surgeon
  • Lyle – Jiffy Lube correspondent and practical witness to Jiffy Bread’s industrial uses
  • Dr. Fritz (Dr. Freudy Enslip) – still trapped in Mini-Freudy mode, practitioner of fun-sized psychology
  • Curtis – candy-powered man-child with teenage ailments and boundless confidence
  • Gigi – teacher of the R-word, arriving just in time to label Curtis’ condition

Sound Stage

  • The Blood Bus – narrow aisles, metallic footsteps, clanking equipment, thin paper cups, squeaky recliners
  • Old Farts Corner – mismatched chairs, cookie shrapnel, diagnostic whispering, and Fred’s ongoing dental trauma
Transcript

Title

John: There she is—the Red Rover of Regret.

Fred: Yep. The only vehicle in town where both the brakes, and the passenger’s squeal

John: That’s … Comforting. … C’mon, Farts …Let’s get our free coffee before the Red Cross gets here.

[bus pulls up and away]

Hope: There’s an extra little pep in John’s step today—an unsettling sight, considering John usually moves like someone glued his joints together with expired honey. … But according to him, something extraordinary has happened. … After what he describes as weeks of intense surveillance of the squirrel population down at Maple Gump Park - Fred calls it ‘loitering’ - John now believes he’s unlocked a brand-new ability…. He claims he can read people the same way he reads those deranged little tree goblins: by watching ‘subtle physical clues’ the untrained eye would miss. … And ever since sunrise, he’s been insisting that Fred address him by his new title.

Fred: Clue-dini! … He wants me to call him Clue-dini ‘cause he thinks he can guess medical conditions.

John: It’s not guessing, Fred. … The clues talk to me. They whisper.

Hope: Sadly, the clues only whisper to John. … The rest of us hear nothing but misplaced confidence and a misguided belief that he is channeling the next Web MD. … But he’s convinced—utterly, absolutely convinced—that every shuffle of a shoe, every scratch of an elbow, every weary Tuesday sigh contains diagnostic secrets waiting for his newly awakened powers. … And so, with Fred rolling his eyes, John scanning every passing stranger like a bargain-bin fortune teller, the boys make their way toward the Old Farts Corner It’s a pair of mismatched recliners shoved together like the world’s saddest diagnostic booth—confident that destiny, free coffee, and poor decisions await.

[2400 characters to get to about 4 min, 10 sec]

John: Ahh… Old Farts Corner. Nothin’ beats luxury upholstery salvaged from the county dump.

Fred: Beats sittin’ on the blood-pressure machine again. That thing pinched me in places I ain’t proud of.

Intro

[SFX: hurried footsteps → Dr. Bobby rushes in]

Dr. Bobby: Comrades! Hello! I am here! Is good to see Booth Brothers alive and… mostly upright!

John: Morning, Doc.

Fred: Hey there, Doctor Bobby. You look unusually energized. New vitamins?

Dr. Bobby: No! I had three espressos and one questionable muffin. I feel like lightning in a lab coat!

John: Well before you electrocute anyone, what’s today’s brew? Smells like burnt ambition with a hint of sorrow.

Dr. Bobby: Is perfect! Today is “Percolated Pulse Blend.” Guaranteed to wake heart, lungs, and one kidney. Now— Let’s get your blood properly caffeinated!

[SFX: coffee pouring into thin paper cups]

[rustle of paper cups for cookies]

Fred: Doc…I’m just looking over today’s cookie selection … these cookies look… dusty.

John: Huh? What?

Fred: No, John, What I mean is … these cookies look … dusty.

Dr. Bobby: Is ok! They are good for platelet morale. Keep blood cells happy during donation. Very scientific.

John: Scientific? Doc, these things are so dry they could sand a picnic table.

[SFX: cookie crunching, Fred coughing on crumbs]

Fred: Mercy… I think one just cut me.

Dr. Bobby: Comrade! So sorry. … My cookies suffer as I spend all time lately on secret rye bread research.

John: Oh yeah, how’s that research on your new rye bread comin’ along?

Dr. Bobby: Ah! Yes. The Rye Initiative progresses. I have assembled elite team of scientists… all highly trained hobbyists to this bakery project.

Fred: Hobbyists?

Dr. Bobby: Yes! Men and women with long track records… mostly in basements… making experimental bread. We call them… The Dough Division.

John: The Dough Division?

Dr. Bobby: Yes! The Dough Division work on breakthrough formula for variant of dense rye bread.

Fred: Dense? Let me guess … More bread designed to stop projectiles?

Dr. Bobby: Nein! This new variant is coming along beautifully. … We work toward formula so dense—so mighty—that one single bite will drop your intelligence by forty IQ points. … Poof! Just gone! … And you do not get them back until bread finally digests… sometime next Thursday. … Lyle segment

Hello, Doctor Bobby

[Lyle walks up]

[SFX: boots clomping up the metal bus aisle]

Lyle: Mornin’, fellas. Doc. Heard y’all talkin’ about that new rye concoction you’re tinkerin’ with. Gotta say — that older batch you gave us last month? Workin’ great down at the Jiffy Lube.

Fred: Ohhh — you mean the Jiffy Bread?

Lyle: Sure do. Stuff’s a miracle. We toss a slice under an oil leak, and the whole thing just… gives up.

John: Hang on now — what in the Cornhole is Jiffy Bread?

Hope: Jiffy Bread” is one of Doctor Bobby’s lesser-known creations — a war-time rye variant he claims to have invented for field medics. … According to his stories, it was designed to help sop up spills in M.A.S.H. units, everything from iodine to coffee to whatever fluid soldiers were unfortunate enough to drop on the floor. … Over the years, that recipe got… repurposed. … When the Greasy Boys down at the Jiffy Lube learned this dense, thirsty loaf could absorb oil, coolant, brake fluid, and the occasional mystery drip, they adopted it immediately. … They now swear by it — for cleanup, for leveling wobbly tool carts, and once, reportedly, as a temporary jack stand.

John: So lemme get this straight… Bobby’s battle bread went from savin’ lives to savin’ garage floors?

Lyle: That’s right. Multi-purpose loaf. Patriotic and practical.

John: Well I’ll be… Clue-dini did not see that one comin’.

Hope: And for once, he wasn’t wrong.

[SFX: Lyle shifts his weight, boot scuff, slight grunt]

John: But hold up now… hold up. Lyle, don’t move. I’m pickin’ up signals.

Fred: Oh boy. Here we go. Dusty’s brain just switched over to “Mystic Chiropractor.

John: I’m serious! Look at him. See that limp on the right side? And that little hitch when he breathes? And he keeps rubbin’ his wrist like he’s makin’ a wish.

Fred: Or maybe he’s just… standin’ there.

John: Shhh! Clue-dini is analyzing. Lyle, you’ve got: … One — a strained hip flexor from liftin’ something heavy yesterday. … Two — that breathing hitch? That’s leftover irritation from inhalin’ too much brake dust. … And three — your wrist is sore ‘cause one of your grandkids twisted it while playin’ “rocket ship” last weekend.

Lyle: Well I’ll be dipped in diesel… That’s exactly what I came to talk to the doc about.

Fred: WHAT?! No. No. No. We are not feedin’ this man’s ego today.

Dr. Bobby: Ja! These are classic Lyle ailments. Very common in Lithuania, where I am from.

Lyle: Very astute, Dusty. … Now, Doc, you got any remedies?

Dr. Bobby: Of course! For hip flexor: you rub with warm cabbage leaf until leaf becomes sad and floppy. For brake-dust lungs: inhale steam from pot of boiled potatoes and dill. And for wrist. … Sleep with wrist buried in bowl of uncooked millet. … Is ancient practice. … Actually … It might be something my neighbor invented.

Lyle: Can’t say I’ve tried millet therapy, but I ain’t above experimentin’. … Thanks, Doc. And Dusty… uh… Clue-dini… appreciate the read.

John: Anytime. The clues never lie.

Fred: Pfft! They lie all the time. You’re just lucky today.

[SFX: Lyle walking away, boots fading]

Hope: And with Lyle patched up through a mixture of guesswork and questionable folk medicine, the bus aisle was briefly safe again… but not for long. Doctor Freudy segment

[Tiny engine sputter approaching → Dr. Fritz puttering up in the mini car]

Hope: For up it’s crowded aisle did roll destiny on four wheels no larger than drink coasters. … It is Doctor Fritz, still in his miniature car … still in his tiny Freudy costume. Doctor Freudy segment

[SFX: tiny engine sputtering → puttering → whining to a stop]

Hope: And just like that, destiny rolled in on four wheels no larger than drink coasters.

Dr. Fritz: Goot morning, gentlemen! It is I—Doctor Freudy—still very much alive and… compact.

John: Freddie my boy, stand back. Time for Clue-dini to strut his stuff.

Fred: Oh please don’t—

John: Now… let’s see. Yes… uh-huh… interesting… Look at that posture, that breathing, that— I diagnose… uh… compression syndrome!

Fred: Dusty… he’s still in the tiny Freudy costume. We can see that.

Dr. Fritz: Ja! I am stuck in zis thing! Very glued in here. Candy bars melted at Trick-or-Treat Transit Loop. I am stuck like traumatized pretzel.

Fred: Yeah, Doc… that chocolate and nougat created some kind of industrial-grade adhesive.

Dr. Bobby: Do not fear! I have medical device for… de-glueing. Special tool. Very advanced.

Hope: And quick as a hiccup, Doctor Bobby reached into his left sock and produced a long, shimmering instrument. … Something between a ceremonial probe and a discount lightsaber.

[SFX: sock rustle → metallic twang → faint whoosh]

John: Doc… what is that?

Dr. Bobby: Is Precision Multifunction Extraction Probe! Passed down through generations of… well… neighbors. Very trusted.

John: And you’re just… waving that around raw?

Dr. Bobby: Of course not! I sterilize now.

[SFX: Bobby taking deep breath, then two dramatic HUFF HUFF puffs]

John: Sterilized?! Doc — you just huffed on it with your breath!

Dr. Bobby: Ja! Because I have been chewing peppermint-licorice gum for last three weeks. Is ancient technique. Passed down by uncle who was… …well… not really a doctor.

Hope: Shocking absolutely no one.

Dr. Bobby: Now — stand back! I shall energize ze probe!

[SFX: bizarre activation noise — part buzz, part chime, part cartoon lightning]

Dr. Fritz: Vait—vait—VAAAIIIIT—!

[SFX: pop → whoomp → rubber-band snap → mini puff of air]

[SFX: suddenly normal-size footsteps staggering]

Dr. Fritz: Ack! I am free! My limbs! They are mine again!

John: Well I’ll be— Clue-dini didn’t see that coming either.

Dr. Bobby: Doctor Fritz! You must not misuse candy! Chocolate is delicious treat, not structural adhesive!

Dr. Fritz: Ja… I know. I go now. Must… process zis shame. Vith snacks.

[SFX: footsteps away; wrapper crinkle as he exits]

Hope: And with Doctor Freudy restored to full size—physically if not emotionally—the bus grew briefly calmer… but only by accident. … Candy talk

Dr. Bobby: Comrades, you would not believe the places I have removed candy since Halloween. Is epidemic of sugar-based emergencies.

Talk Turns to Candy

John: Oh this oughta be good.

Dr. Bobby: First, small boy swallowed plastic spider ring. I had to extract it from his nostril. Nostril! Ring traveled up somehow. Like salmon swimming upstream.

Fred: Doc… that’s not how noses work.

Dr. Bobby: You tell spider ring that. … Then! At Maple Grove Library, teenager used candy corn as “earplugs. … Earplugs! … When they melted, his ears were sealed shut. I had to steam them open. … Very dramatic. Many witnesses fainted. … Mostly me.

Fred: Yeah, I’ve always claimed that candy corn is the most traumatic of Halloween candies.

Dr. Bobby: And our friend Jimmy—ah, poor Jimmy—he lined his winter hat with peppermint patties ‘for insulation.’ The heat from his skull fused them into a minty armor plate. Now everyone wants to lick his head like he’s some kind of portable dessert.

Hope: Speaking of candy… look who’s arriving now, as if summoned by the very mention of a sugar-induced disaster.

[Curtis runs up]

[SFX: rapid sneakers, hurried footsteps, slight skid]

Curtis: Candy? Whoa baby! You talkin’ candy? Nut-Zilla and I cleaned up! Kids were droppin’ their bags and runnin’ for their lives soon as he revved those nut-crackers! Looked like a parade… if the parade was terrified.

John: Curtis, stand still. Clue-dini is receiving… signals. Mmm-hmm… yes… oh, that’s unusual— I diagnose early-stage appendicitis, a flare of tonsillitis, and an acne outbreak I haven’t seen since the Eighth-Grade Pizza Panic of ’03.

Fred: Dusty? You sure? … those are all teenager problems.

John: Exactly! Which makes Curtis a medical mystery.

Dr. Bobby: Ja! These ailments are typically found in juvenile specimens aged twelve to sixteen. Curtis… you are forty-two. How do you have biology of middle-school locker room?

Curtis: Pshh—please. Of course I get tonsillitis every year. … It’s the only way I get free ice cream and miss choir practice. … You so dumb!

Dr. Bobby: But appendicitis twice in one decade? And acne pattern consistent with “first crush on social-studies teacher”? Very strange.

Curtis: Look, if your acne don’t show up twice a year, you’re not livin’ right.

[Curtis runs off]

Hope: And with that sage advice, Curtis rocketed away, proud as ever, unaware that his adolescent field of acne was moments from evolving into a respectable middle-age rash. R word for the Day

R Word of the Day

Gigi: Rash? Did someone say Rash? Hey kids! That’s our R word for the day! Can you say rash? Rash! Rash! Very Good. Now in a sentence please!

Fred: Rash. R.A.C.H.E. Rash! It’s when you do somethin’ foolish and gotta go down to the crick and rash it off. Rash!

[R word chime out]

[Aria walks up]

Hope: And just when the bus aisle thought it had recovered from Curtis’s hormonal hurricane… here came Aria, armed with sass, attitude, and zero tolerance for nonsense.

Aria: Alright, boys… Straighten up, and start actin’ your age. … Or whatever age you’re pretending to be today.

Fred: We make no promises.

Aria: I’m just glad to see you’re getting your coffee reservoirs properly filled. Means I might have a fairly normal day down at the Lounge today…

Fred: Not with Clue-dini lurking about.

John: Wait, wait, wait—Aria, don’t move. Clue-dini senses… something. Your walk… your glow… the way you’re holdin’ your right wrist. Aria… I think you might be… pregnant.

[Beat of dead silence]

Aria: Umm, no! … I tripped on a crack, spilled hot coffee on my skin and twisted my wrist trying to get out of the way! JON! What is WRONG with you?! I had a hysterectomy last year! You—YOU—were the one who drove me home from the hospital!

Fred: Uhhh… he did. You stole a wheelchair, too.

Aria: YES! … John, you wheeled me down the wrong hallway, crashed into a vending machine, and shouted, “SHE’LL WALK IT OFF!” … You remember none of this?!

John: Well… I remember the vending machine part.

Gigi: Uh… hi! So… funny story. Aria’s not pregnant.

Fred: Yeah, we’re real clear on that now.

Gigi: Seriously John … You didn’t notice the waddle? The fact that my shirt doesn’t cover my belly any more? The baby shower your wife had for me at your very own house? ... Clue-dini? Yeah, right! More like Inspector Cclouseau I’m the one who’s pregnant!

John: Oh

Fred: Wow! Well hot dog on a pogo stick! Congratulations, Gigi!

Aria: Gigi! Congratulations girl!

Dr. Bobby: Comrades! Do not worry! I, Doctor Bobby, can deliver baby. I have delivered many goats. This will be very similar. Probably. Maybe. We see.

Aria: ABSOLUTELY NOT.

Gigi: Yeah, I’m gonna use a real doctor. No offense, Bobby… but your last medical device came out of your sock.

Dr. Bobby: None taken. I shall instead work on rye crib of bread

Aria: Good idea, Doc!

John: Well… Farts, I think that’s our cue.

Fred: Yep. When Bobby starts designing furniture outta bread, it’s time to go.

[SFX: footsteps fading as they head off the bus]

Hope: And so John and Fred wandered off, leaving behind a bus full of confusion, crumbs, and one very confident goat-midwife. … Tuesdays in Maple Grove rarely end where they begin, and today was no exception. … Some days the town feels held together by duct tape, bad decisions, and whatever Dr. Bobby keeps in his socks. … From Clue-dini diagnoses to candy extractions to surprise announcements, the blood bus once again proved itself the least predictable ride in town.
