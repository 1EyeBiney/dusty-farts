Episode 11: Condiment Clash

Ep. 11 — Condiment Clash

Chapters

Summary

The Polyester Lounge turns into a battlefield of bottles when John (Dusty) and Fred (Farts) miss their blood-bus appointment and arrive hangry. One wrong swirl of ketchup ignites the Great Condiment War — a full-scale tabletop skirmish complete with sliding ramekins, tactical relish, and mustard militias.

Hope narrates as Aria fights to keep the peace (and her sanity), while Fred invents a “scientific” rating system called the Z.E.S.T.Y. Scale — the Zero-Bias Estimation of a Sauce’s and Topping’s Yummyness. Ketchup becomes democracy, mustard becomes tyranny, and soy sauce sneaks in like a salty saboteur. By the end, the table’s a Jackson Pollock of sauces, the boys are humbled, and Aria walks away with new shoes — and no pie for them.

It’s a sticky, salty, sweet-and-sour serving— proof that friendship can survive almost anything… except maybe honey mustard.

Voices You’ll Hear

  • John (Dusty) – grizzled philosopher-soldier of the Mustard Militia
  • Fred (Farts) – sentimental ketchup loyalist and creator of the Z.E.S.T.Y. Scale
  • Aria – sarcastic waitress, part referee, part hostage to flavor
  • Hope – narrator and voice of reason in a war of condiments
  • Gigi – R-Word cameo, bringing harmonic punctuation to the chaos

Sound Stage

  • The Polyester Lounge Booth #3 – clinking plates, squirting bottles, the sizzle of fries, and the rhythmic thump of plastic sliding across Formica.

It’s Tuesday after all, and that means Schedule T for “Tuesday, triage, and Trouble.” Because every Tuesday during the vernal equinox must follow the daily routine. To the T.

Opening

John: I told ’em if they just stuffed the tire with a couple jelly donuts from the back row, it’d balance out the sugar pressure.

Hope: The net result of the resulting ten-car traffic jam was that John and Fred missed their visit with Doctor Bobby for coffee, cookies, and their weekly blood cleaning and filtering. It left our pair of pouty principals a couple of pints emptier of vital coffee and a few quarts fuller of unfiltered, argumentative blood of their own. So, just be warned, folks, the cantankerous crew is a little saltier than usual today.

[aria walks up]

Aria: Well hello prune face. And it’s Nice to see you as well Sour Dough. Runnin’ a little late today, aren’t ya boys?

John: Don’t get me started … Aria.

Fred: Well, Toast my kneecaps! …And smother them with Ben Gay. … Can we at least still get a Cup of jitter juice

Aria: Of course, White Bread. I’ll even bring you a couple dozen packets of sugar like you like. But I can’t promise it’s very fresh. It might just crawl right out of your cup. Now. What you two pioneers want for lunch? You know Bob has added quite a few selections to the menu lately.

How crispy?

John: I swear, if I hear you say “extra crispy” one more time, I’m gonna stick a fork in the outlet just to show you I’m extra crispy.

Fred: Well, that’s just fine … as long as Bob salts you properly afterward.

John: Farts! … What are we gonna do with you? … I think we’re gonna need an intervention on …

Fred: Salt. Yeah, I know, salt. … Everybody is always getting on me about salt.

John: Actually … salt’s sittin’ at number nine on the list, just above dental floss.

[Aria walks up, balancing a tray.]

Aria: Here you go, fellas — some freshly recycled coffee. Bob says it’s brewed to perfection, which around here means it’s been reheated at least twice and strained through yesterday’s grits.

Fred: Thanks, Aria. Can always count on you to serve caffeine with a side of optimism.

Aria: I don’t know about Optimism around here. … Honey, that pot’s darker than Bob’s tax history.

Fred: Yeah, I don’t know if it’s proper to count burnt bacon as depreciation. … Now, if you don’t mind, please settle a little disagreement between Dusty and myself.

Aria: No way. Not if it’s about food. I’ve seen where that road leads — gravy riots, toast debates, and a waffle incident I still can’t talk about without twitching.

Fred: Now, c’mon, Aria, it’s nothin’ dramatic. We just need your opinion about the true nature of salsa.

Aria: That’s what I thought. No way. I’m not getting caught in another condiment crossfire. Last time I had to mop up coleslaw like it was a crime scene. Now, if you’ll excuse me, I’ve got fries to overcook and Bob’s dreams to underseason.

[Aria walks off, muttering about “hazard pay.”]

John: Alright, Farts. I know you’ve been working on this for a while. Let’s hear your opening remarks.

Fred: If it please the court… and by court, I mean everything west of the cash register. … , I’d like to present my case: Is salsa really a condiment, or is it a side?

Hope: According to Fred, wizened scholars for years have wrestled with the great universal question: is salsa a condiment, a side, or an overachieving tomato trying to be both? … Fred claims to have cracked the code somewhere between the 2002 Super Bowl party and a regrettable midnight snacklater that same year.

Fred: See, I say if it spreads, it’s a condiment. But if it scoops, it’s a side. That’s the law of the land.

John: The “law of the land”? What, did the Supreme Court rule on burritos when I wasn’t lookin’?

Fred: I’m just saying, Dusty — salsa don’t spread. You scoop it, dip it, or drown it. Ain’t nobody butterin’ toast with salsa.

John: That’s because sane people exist, Fred. … Look … You can dip fries in mayo, but that don’t make mayo a religion.

Fred: Speak for yourself. I’ve been attending those gatherings for a while now. … We meet every Thursday down at the Fellowship Hall of Flavor.

Hope: And so continued this endless debate over the nature and qualities of salsa. … Here, I’ll give you the … umm … highlights, from over the years. … In 2005, John tried to prove salsa was a condiment by slathering it on Bob’s famous Tuesday pancakes. The result was an emergency evacuation, two broken spatulas, and the only documented salsa fire in diner history. …

Aria: I was here for that one. Bob spent three hours explaining to the insurance guy how ‘salsa fire’ counts as a random act of condiment.

Hope: Then in 2011, John doubled down during the “Great Nacho Summit” of Maple Grove. … Unfortunately, details are a little hazy as it is a little talked about event in town history, but we know that it ended with John being escorted out of the VFW for “excessive enthusiasm with a ladle.” …

Fred: Well, if you ask me, the Nacho Summit went fine — up until Dusty declared independence from guacamole.

Hope: But Fred? Fred’s no saint either. … His 2018 revelation came when he tried to serve salsa as his main course at the church chili cookoff. … He called it “Tex-Mex Soup of the Soul.

John: Kids are still scraping tomato chunks off the hymnals.

Hope: Sometimes the arguing carries well into the buffet hours of dinner time. … And often, it’s only interrupted by random events courtesy of the universe.

John: Look! Squirrel!

Hope: See what I mean?

[Aria walks upwith burgers ]

Aria: Dusty … I swear, you got the attention span of a gnat. … Here’s your burgers, boys — just the way you like ’em. Burnt blacker than the asphalt in the parkimng lot…. And you got all your fixin’s too — ketchup, mustard, pickles, lettuce, and onion.

[She sets the plates down with a clatter.]

Fred: Umm, Aria? … Where’s the ketchup?

Aria: It’s right there. On the bun. I can see three swirls of it.

Fred: You call that little mosquito sneeze of a spill the correct amount of ketchup? Puh-leeze.

John: Yeah, mine needs more mustard.

Aria: Are you kidding me, Dusty? I had Bob put four big dollops on yours. Look at that thing — it’s practically sweating yellow.

John: Yeah, well, it’s not enough.

Fred: Yeah, not enough.

Mustard and Ketchup

Aria: Good grief. You two don’t need a waitress — you need condiment counselors.

[Aria walks off, muttering about needing hazard pay for “ketchup therapy.”]

Hope: And so it began. The day the Polyester Lounge became the battleground for the Great Condiment War. Two men. One booth. Infinite sauces. The first skirmish — Ketchup versus Mustard.

John: Farts, my man. I don’t know why you always carry on about tomato juice. … Ketchup is just baby’s first condiment. Real men enjoy the sting.

Fred: Now Dusty, how dare you talk about democracy in such a fashion! … Mustard’s just anger in a bottle. Ketchup’s the people’s choice — tangy , and unifying!

John: People’s choice? Please. Ketchup’s the training wheels of taste. You outgrow it by the time you can spell “flavor.

Fred: And mustard’s what you use when you’ve given up on joy. That’s not fun, Dusty, that’s regret in a squeeze bottle.

John: Oh yeah? Mustard built civilizations. It’s the condiment of warriors, laborers, and lunchbox heroes.

Fred: Please. Ketchup feeds the masses. Mustard just jolts the unprepared.

John: Jolts? That’s flavor, Fred. That’s experience. Mustard puts hair on your tongue.

Fred: And ketchup? … Ketchup puts a smile on your soul.

John: I see. Well, then … Ketchup puts sugar in your bloodstream and shame in your lunch.

Fred: Uh-huh … While Mustard puts blisters in your sinuses and wounds you permanently.

Hope: It went on like this for several minutes. John, the self-proclaimed general of the Mustard Militia, and Fred, ketchup’s patriotic defender. Two philosophies divided by color and condiment aisle.

[Aria returns, carrying two bottles — one red, one yellow — like a peace offering at a condiment summit.]

Aria: Here — democracy and tyranny in plastic form. Try not to start another civil war, boys.

Hope: But peace is often short-lived in Booth Three…. … Sides had been drawn, and one by one, condiments were pulled like pawns into this Battle Royale. … Aria made no fewer than seven trips to the kitchen, each time returning with another bottle or jar like a weary quartermaster supplying a doomed army. … Spicy pickle relish, wasabi, and horseradish sauce were marshaled under John’s command — Team Hot and Spicy. …

John: Here’s to Horseradish Sauce — nothing says dignity like a root that can clear both your sinuses and your calendar.

Hope: Meanwhile, Fred countered with sweet pickle relish, mayonnaise, and Kansas City–style sweet barbecue sauce.

Fred: The wise elder of the grill. … It doesn’t yell or argue … it simply persuades with sweet molasses.

Hope: Ranch dressing, ever the diplomat, almost brought an end to this condiment conflict.

Fred: The universal peacemaker. Fries, wings, pizza crusts — Ranch will negotiate with anything.

Hope: But just as the room began to calm, Soy Sauce slipped in — dark, unpredictable, and ready to stir up trouble once again.

John: It’s too salty.

Fred: That’s the point.

Aria: I see. … You two can’t agree on anything. … Fine. Here’s one for you geniuses — Honey Mustard.

John: Traitor! … Clearly the Benedict Arnold of mustards!

Fred: Nonsense. … Don’t be an emotional gherkin, Dusty! /… Honey Mustard’s just proof that anything can find love if it sweet-talks enough.

Aria: I… don’t have time for this. If anyone needs me, I’ll be in the kitchen pretending the deep fryer is white noise.

[Aria walks off, muttering something about “emergency butter therapy.”]

John: Let’s see now… three, no, four condiments per side. I see you got your ketchup, mayo, relish, and ranch — your so-called “civilian sauces.” … While I have the adult lineup. Horseradish. Sriracha. Wasabi. Spicy Relish. Real sauces for people with working taste buds.

Fred: Ok, whatever, Generalissimo Jalapeño.

John: But I did notice you set down the ketchup so it’s got a distinct tactical advantage over the salsa.

Pawns in the grand scheme

Fred: That’s ’cause salsa’s a neutral country. Everybody dips, nobody wins.

[Sliding sounds as the bottles, shakers, and ramekins scrape across the tabletop. The two men rearrange their “forces.”]

John: I see your flanking maneuver! I see the way you try to hide your relish with a flanking maneuver along the hot dog peninsula. … bold … but predictable.

Fred: And I can’t help but notice your spicy battalion’s been outnumbered. … You’ve got Wasabi guarding the Nugget Archipelago, but one wrong move and Ranch floods the entire Salad Basin.

John: Maybe so, but my outposts of Sriracha and Horseradish are mobilized. And that Relish Front? Impenetrable.

Fred: Impenetrable? Dusty, that relish is sittin’ in a plastic ramekin. It’s one sneeze away from being scattered to the 4 winds.

John: Ah, the arrogance of Ketchup Command. Overconfident, underseasoned, and already surrounded by napkin casualties.

Fred: At least my mayo militia keeps morale high. Your recruiters are too spicy to recruit new soldiers.

Hope: And so, once again, Booth Three descended into tactical madness. … Over the years, John and Fred have re-created the game of Risk in every way, well, except of course, owning a board. … Here at the Lounge, they’ve mapped battles across the breakfast counter using salt and pepper shakers, and once, drafted sugar packets into armies, calling them “The Sweet Legion.” … During another particularly drawn-out campaign, they annexed Booth Four to their sovereign territory, using only a line of French fries and a stolen Tabasco bottle. … To the untrained eye, it’s coots and chaos. To them? It’s simply tactics and tradition.

John: Checkmate, Farts. Your fries are cut off from supply lines, and I’m fortifying the Nugget Archipelago.

Fred: You wouldn’t dare. That’s a humanitarian zone! … Where will all the innocent ketchup casualties go?

John: Sorry, Farts. … All’s fair in lunch and condiments. stopped

Hope: And thus, the Great Condiment War raged on — fought not with weapons or words, but with squirts, smears, and stains.

[wet squirting noises — the unmistakable sound of plastic bottles reaching their breaking point.]

Aria: What the—?! What is wrong with you two?! Look at this table! It looks like you handed my blind friend Jimmy a palette and asked him to make you an abstract painting of the rainbow.

Fred: Now, Aria, it’s not as bad as it looks—

Aria: Fred! There’s sweet pickle relish floating in your coffee mug.

John: That’s … umm … uhh … a floating tactical garnish?

Aria: That’s … a health code violation. … Why can’t you two just get along like normal people?

Fred: Yeah, you’re right. … We do seem to fight over the silliest of stuff. … So …in this new spirit of cooperation and togetherness, we shall combine forces and create a new system…. … a fair and unbiased rating scale.

Aria: Oh lord.

Fred: Introducing… The Z.E.S.T.Y. Scale!

Hope: A Zero-Bias Estimation of a Sauce’s and Topping’s Yummyness

John: Wait, what? Z.E.S. what what?

Hope: Z., E., S., T., Y. …

Fred: Zesty!

Aria: Are you? … You tellin’ me that you have developed a rating scale on taste that is called ‘Zesty’?

Fred: You got it.

Aria: I see. Well, you two are lunatics. L.U.N.A.T.I.C.s. Loud, Unruly, No-account Arguers Talking In Circles.

Hope: You know … If you step back and think about it. … That Zesty Scale … It’s the kind of thing that sounds scientific until you remember it’s being developed by two men currently covered in salsa.

John: Don’t forget a half-point bonus for any sauce that can remove rust stains or ease marital tension.

Fred: I concur! … and duly noted. … Now, let’s begin with our first assessment. We shall use relish. R word for the day

Gigi: Relish? Did someone say Relish? Hey kids! That’s today’s R word for the day! Can you say relish?

Fred: Relish. … R.E.L.I.S.H. … Relish. that’s when you enjoy peanut butter so much, you smear it on everything … even pancakes.

Gigi: Umm, that’s actually a cry for help, kids!

Fred: Relish.

[R word outro]

Hope: The new Zesty ratings system was as sound as their friendship — sticky, subjective, and one shake away from full frontal food fury. … Eventually, even neutral Parmesan was unwillingly pulled into the fray, which ended with a blanket of grated cheese covering everything on the table.

Aria: Congratulations, boys. You just turned lunch into a food fight without moving a muscle. … And you didn’t even eat any of your food!

[John and Fred exchange guilty glances.]

John: Uhh, yeah. … Well … sorry about the table.

Fred: Yeah, I’m sorry. … But we did get you somethin’.

Aria: Uh-huh … And if it’s up under that snowbank of parmesan, then I don’t want it.

John: No, no…. You’ll like ‘em.

Fred: Yeah, Mitsi down at Stout’s Shoes said you been eyin’ these Skechers Arch Fit Slip-Ins for a while.

[They hand over a paper bag. Aria opens it, surprised to find a sleek, supportive pair of slip-on shoes.]

Aria: Well, I’ll be… You right. I’ve been readin’ about them. … They are comfy. … I mean, you could’ve given them to me before I walked ten miles fetching condiments for you two, but still. … You two actually did something thoughtful Thank you, John. Thank you, Fred.

Fred: Don’t mention it.

Aria: I won’t. … Long as you two idjits clean up this here mess! … Oh! And today … you two ain’t getting no pie.

John: Wait! What?

Fred: Ohhhhh nooooo!

[aria walks off]

Hope: And so ended another day at the Polyester Lounge — the table sticky, the air thick with regret and vinegar, It ended with two men, humbled and sentenced to hard labor…. Because in this world, kids, … when you don’t play nice…… You get sent home without any pie.

[Harmonica riff. Booth fades to silence.]
