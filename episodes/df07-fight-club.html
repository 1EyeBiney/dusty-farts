Episode 7: Fight Club

September 19, 2025 · 16 min

Ep. 7 — Fight Club

Download MP3 (15 MB)

Chapters

Summary

Back at the Polyester Lounge, John (Dusty) and Fred (Farts) discover that Bob has found new inspiration from an Internet meme. His latest scheme? Hosting a full-on Parking Lot Pugilism Pageant — complete with Marquis of Sheepsberry rules, goat referees, and Steve pressed into service as a carnival-style announcer.

As always, Aria serves sass alongside coffee while the boys argue over thermostat settings that somehow escalate into a literal goat-punching contest. With bleats as the official scorecard and “gentleman’s temperature” as the hill to die on, the Polyester Lounge transforms into the most ridiculous fight club the Midwest has ever seen.

Voices You’ll Hear

  • John (Dusty) – thermostat crusader, brawler by default, and defender of 74 degrees as “the gentleman’s temperature”
  • Fred (Farts) – coupon king, snack philosopher, and a man whose best punch might be a goat-slap
  • Aria – waitress, sassmaster, and unwilling referee to farm-animal fisticuffs
  • Steve – over-the-top carnival announcer forced into emcee duty
  • Hope – narrator, conspiratorial tour guide, and reluctant witness to the nonsense

Sound Stage

  • The Polyester Lounge – cracked vinyl booths, clinking mugs, and Aria’s coffee pot patrol
  • The Parking Lot – echoing announcer mic, goat bleats, exaggerated slow-motion punches, and the faint hum of diner neon
Transcript

Title

Hope: Previously, on Dusty Farts. … John and Fred survived Curtis’s sass, graded sawdust coffee from an animatronic gnome, enjoyed brine from a sample cup, and nearly caffeinated themselves into oblivion. … But even the most heroic explorers need rest and recovery, and all roads lead back to the Polyester Lounge. … It’s home turf for cracked vinyl, some good brew, all Aria’s sass, and sometimes … Many times, … Bob’s hair-brained ideas.

[Harmonica wipe]

[Door opens]

John: Glory be, Farts. … That door is louder than your bed under the covers after all-you-can-eat chili night down at the Lion’s Club.

Fred: That’s called civic Pride, Dusty. … Every single toot is a fine salute to the vast duties of Lion’s Club booties.

John: Duty, booty, or tooty — I don’t care. … Booth three’s a callin’, but I swear there’s a buzz in here today. Feels like Bob’s cookin’’ up more than bacon.

Hope: Dusty is right. … Bob is often prone to hair-brained ideas, and, like his most frequent customers, John and Fred, has trouble differentiating advertising from Internet memes. It’s a problem Aria has battled for years, going back to when Bob was inspired by a meme to serve Tide Pods as dessert samples. So, Aria knew they were in trouble when Bob fixated on one recent joke about his biggest rival. The Waffle House.

John: Oh, no. … Not, “the House.

Hope: Yes. The House. Nothing gets Bob’s attention more than when Waffle House Management makes a change. … In fact, Bob treats each change to menu, signage or operating hours like a key strategic move in the ninth inning of game 7 of the World Series.

Intro

Aria: Yeah, right. … Only part of Bob like Yankees manager Billy Martin is the temper tantrums in the dirt at home plate.

Hope: So, when a meme started circulating the Internet with a joke about how Waffle House management has decided to bring some class to their joint by cleaning up the fisticuffs, Bob was instantly in full inspiration mode. In the meme, it was suggested that fighters were now forced to follow Marquis of Queensberry rules, you know, timed rounds, gloved hands, no hitting below the belt, in an effort to try and control the carnage. Supposedly, these midnight melees got so unruly that the only solution was Victorian-style decorum.

[sit down sounds]

[Aria walks up]

Fred: It’s the gentlemanly thing to do, naturally.

Aria: Yep, except Bob has taken this to a whole new level.

John: Oh, Hi there Aria

Aria: Dusty. Farts. … I’d say welcome back, but that implies someone actually missed you.

Fred: Why thank you, Aria. Always a joy to be greeted like a stubbed toe at midnight. … Now, please, do go on.

Aria: Thank you, White Bread. Anyway—like I was sayin’… Bob’s decided to put his own spin on that meme. He’s now encouraging fights right here in the Polyester Lounge parking lot.

John: Oh, Lord…

Aria: Yep… and he’s callin’ it… The Polyester Lounge Parking Lot Pugilism Pageant. … proudly presented by the Marquis of Sheepsberry.

Fred: The Marquis?

John: Of Sheepsberry?

Aria: That’s right. You know that little village out on the western edge of town?

John: Course I do. I grew up on Sheepsberry Hill. Still got cousins up there who think banjo lessons count as higher education.

Aria: Well, according to Bob, there’s this fella up there calls himself ‘The Marquis.’ And apparently, he’s drawn up his very own set of fightin’ rules.

Fred: Well, I’ll be wobblier than a three-legged dog on a Slip ’N Slide…. My interest is officially piqued. … So tell me, what’s the rules?

Aria: To be honest? Still a little foggy. Bob’s out back scribblin’ ‘em down on a napkin between bacon burns.

John: Rules or no rules, I’m ready for some post-brunch fireworks in the parking lot.

Aria: So’s the rest of the breakfast crowd. … Bob slipped a little extra hot sauce into the scrambled eggs this mornin’. They’re all sittin’ here hotter than a thermostat with anger issues. Thermostat Issues

Fred: Speakin’ of heat, Dusty — I noticed you cranked the thermostat in the car all the way up to seventy-four on the drive over.

John: Of course. … It’s a fine breezy afternoon. Great day to get my whole 10-minutes of sunshine.

Fred: Ten minutes? Dusty, at seventy-four degrees in that car, I was sweatin’ like a hog at a county fair. Felt like you was tryin’ to slow-roast me on the way over.

John: Nonsense. Seventy-four is the gentleman’s temperature. Perfect balance of comfort and dignity.

Fred: Gentleman’s temperature? More like a crockpot setting. If I’d been in there five more minutes, you’d be bastin’ me with gravy.

John: Well, maybe if you didn’t keep droppin’ it to sixty-eight like we’re haulin’ ice cream, I wouldn’t have to bump it back up. Seventy-four is civilized.

Fred: Jumpin’ Jehoshophat! … Civilized? Dusty, seventy-four is a sauna. Felt like you were tryin’ to poach me on the way over. I’m pretty sure it says in Psalms 68, “Blessed are those … that find rest at 68.

John: Please. Sixty-eight freezes the marrow. Seventy-four keeps the blood movin’. There’s a stark difference.

Aria: Lord, you two argue more about degrees than a room full of college dropouts.

Fred: Tell you what, Dusty. If you’re so sure you’re right, let’s settle this like men.

[fred stands up]

John: You mean—

Fred: That’s right. Parking lot. Now.

[John starts walking]

[John stands up]

John: Fine by me. Been meanin’ to knock some sense into you since the great toast debate of ’97.

[John walks off]

Hope: Umm, guys? … Umm, Aria? … This … this isn’t in the script! …

[rustle papers]

Aria: Oh, Lord. Here we go. Somebody cue the harmonica.

[door]

[harmonica sting]

[new ambient parking lot]

Steve: Welcome! Ladies, gentlemen, and livestock of all ages! You’ve stumbled into history in the makin’ — the very first Polyester Lounge Parking Lot Pugilism Pageant! Proudly — and I do mean proudly — presented under the world-famous Marquis of Sheepsberry Rules!

Parking Lot Pagent

Hope: This is Steve. Don’t ask. His résumé? … Normally, he’s the booming loudspeaker voice at the Maple Grove Seasonal Carnival. And by ‘seasonal,’ I mean they rotate every 3 months between four abandoned car lots. … one north, one south, one east, one west. … Same rides, same funnel cakes, same suspicious Tilt-A-Whirl. … They just swap costumes and slap on a new banner so the town thinks they’re four different carnivals. They’re not.

Steve: North, south, east, west — no matter the lot, you’ll still leave queasy!

[SFX: faint carnival organ riff under Hope, fading out]

Hope: Anyway… Steve lost a bet with Bob at last year’s county fair. Something about a greased pig contest. I don’t know all the details, but Bob swears the pig was double-parked and Steve swears the rules were ‘slippery.’ Either way, Steve’s been stuck ever since — half security guard, half valet, full-time regret — right here in the Polyester Lounge parking lot.

Steve: I maintain, ladies and gentlemen, that was an exhibition hog — not fit for regular-season competition!

Hope: But tonight? Tonight Bob’s thrown him a bone. If Steve plays Master of Ceremonies for this ridiculous Pugilism Pageant, he shaves six months off his parking lot sentence. Which explains why he’s leaning so hard into this whole boxing-announcer routine.

Steve: That’s right, folks! Stick around! Nobody leaves disappointed… unless you ordered the coleslaw. Then you’re on your own.

Aria: Hey, Steve? … You do realize that everyone is still sittin’ inside, just lookin’ out the window at us. … This parking lot is emptier than Dusty’s wallet.

Steve: I know. But Bob wants a show. … So, Bob’s getting a show. And tonight, ladies, gentlemen and livestock of all ages! … prepare yourselves for the Polyester Lounge’s one and only, officially unsanctioned, entirely regrettable Pugilism Pageant!

Steve: John. … Fred. … Prepare yourselves! … The Polyester Lounge Parking Lot is your stage! … And your pride is the only prize! But based on the sight of these two Dusty Farts, it’s fair to say.… Let’s Get ready to GRUMBLE!! Today’s R Word

Gigi: Grumble? … Hey kids! That’s awful close to today’s ‘R’ word of the Day. … Kids? Can you say ‘Rumble’?

Fred: Rumble. R.U.M.B.E.L.L. … Rumble. The sounds you make when you try complainin’ without your dentures in. … Rumble.

[R word outro]

Steve: Speaking of denture-less wonders … Let’s introduce our two

Steve: Ladies, gentlemen and livestock of all ages! … I give you… our first contender! … Hailing from the western side of booth number 3 at the Polyester Lounge. … The man who once argued a traffic cone into submission! … The Sultan of Side-Eye, the Mayor of Miralax, the Maestro of Mild Hypertension… John. … Smith, I guess.

John: Hypertension? I’m fine. Doctor Bobby says my blood pressure’s just ‘creatively elevated.

Aria: Yeah, Dusty. You’re basically a geyser with sideburns.

Steve: And in the opposite corner, wearing nothing but misplaced confidence and yesterday’s gravy stain… He’s the King of Coupons! … The Baron of Biscotti! … The only man who thinks Snausages is its own food group… Give it up for… F.A.R.T.S.! Farts!!

[crowd chant we want farts]

Fred: Thank you kindly. It’s true. I once wrestled a family-sized meatloaf to a draw.

Aria: Yeah, but the meatloaf still won on presentation. stopped

Steve: Together… they are the booth-bound brawlers, the polyester pugilists, the cranky combatants you didn’t order but got served anyway! … John and Fred — the Dusty Farts!!

[mild crowd cheer]

Hope: Absurd? Absolutely. But then again, this is the Polyester Lounge. Absurd comes free with every refill! … Oh! And this is so far now off the rails. … Y’all are on your own! … At least till Brian needs me to explain something.

John: Ok, fine. So, what’s the rules for this little skirmish?

[crinkly paper sound]

Steve: Yes, here they are. … Each contestant will be allowed to pick one of the prized goats of the Marquis of Sheepsberry. … You will then present the next statement in your argument followed by a punch of your opponent’s goat. You start with 3 points, but if you fail to make the goat bleat , you lose a point. The fight lasts until you lose all your points or you knock out your opponent’s goat. … Either with logic or a blow. The Fight

John: Punchin’ goats, huh? That’s… not exactly Marquis of Queensberry.

Aria: Nope. This is strictly Marquis of Sheepsberry. Comes with a lot more bleatin’ and a lot less dignity.

Steve: Very well! Contestant number one — step forth! Present your argument, deliver your blow, and await the blessed bleat! Round 1 – Fred whiffs

Sheepsberry Rules

Fred: Dusty… your thermostat settings are hotter than a jalapeño funeral.

[SFX: wild punch → air woosh → NO contact]

Steve: OH! A devastating strike to… absolutely nothing! The goat remains unmoved! … Contestant loses a point!

[negative ding sound]

Aria: Farts! You got worse aim than my blind friend Jimmy! Round 2 – John, normal punch

Steve: Ok, sultan of swat. … You’re next! … Go ahead and make your case. … then try to hit the goat’s face.

John: Sixty-eight degrees, Farts, is for penguins and pallbearers. … Seventy-four is for court and kings!

[SFX: punch with goat bleat]

Aria: Majestic. Truly. Nothing screams royalty like slappin’ a goat into agreement. Round 3 – Fred stoic goat

Fred: Fine! Take this, ya thermostat tyrant!

[SFX: solid punch sound → SILENCE]

[negative sound]

Steve: Ahh… the goat has chosen stoic silence. No bleat, 1 pointdeduction! … Contestant remains… tragically goatless.

Aria: Fred, honey, I’ve seen stronger swings from a screen door in a light breeze. Round 4 – John normal

John: Farts, you couldn’t set a thermostat if the only options were ‘ON’ and ‘FROSTBITE.

[SFX: solid punch → goat bleat]

Steve: And the goat cries out! Dusty connects once again! stopped

Aria: Great. Now we got farm animals validating his ego. Round 5 – Fred with soft slap

Fred: Alright, Dusty… if I can’t win with power, I’ll win with finesse. Behold— the gentle Touch of justice!

[SFX: open-hand SLAP → soft baby goat bleat]

Aria: Lord above, Farts— did you just spank a petting-zoo reject? That sounded like lamb daycare.

Steve: Indeed! A softer strike… but a bleat nonetheless! … No point deduction. Round 6

John: Prepare yourself, Fred! … for the gentleman’s temperature… of DESTINY.

[SFX: punch begins normally → last portion slowed to 0.5 playback → stretched, echoing goat bleat]

Steve: And there it is! … A thunderous, time-bending, goat-shattering blow! … Ladies and gentlemen, behold — your undisputed Champion of Sheepsberry!

Aria: Yeah, congrats, Dusty. You just won Bob’s big grand prize…

John: Oh yeah? ‘Bout time! … What is it?

Steve: A coupon for Bob’s chili! … valid only on the fifth Monday of any month. … and a whole case of Bob’s special hot sauce, with an expiration date of… yesterday.

John: So… basically, I just knocked out a goat for the privilege of getting food poisoning.
