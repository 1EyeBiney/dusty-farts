Episode 8: Jiffy Part Deaux

October 6, 2025 · 14 min

Ep. 8 — Jiffy Part Deaux

Download MP3 (13 MB)

Chapters

Summary

The Jiffy Lube booth isn’t just a booth anymore—it’s a battlefield. When Curtis tags their vinyl with Sharpie graffiti, John (Dusty) and Fred (Farts) declare war on turf encroachment, only to get dragged into rivalries stranger than a squirrel staring contest. Lyle gets saddled with a new nickname, Curtis fires off insults with GPS precision, and even the grease boys become unwilling competitors in an Olympic-style filter ballet.

Somewhere between betting on postal carriers like racehorses, debating thermostat truces, and critiquing rookie mechanics as if the fate of the free world depended on torque angles, Fred makes a surprising gesture: handing off his “smart” watch to someone who needs it more. Hope calls it random, but it’s anything but—because behind the sarcasm and sludge, Dusty and Farts still manage to deliver a little heart along with the burnt coffee.

Voices You’ll Hear

  • John (Dusty) – defender of booth honor, master of pickle-jar metaphors
  • Fred (Farts) – reluctant philanthropist, thermostat negotiator, postal bookie
  • Hope – narrator, weary of turf wars and unexpected generosity
  • Curtis – sassmaster, booth tagger, professional thorn in their side
  • Lyle – gatekeeper of sludge, accidental referee of booth disputes

Sound Stage

  • Jiffy Lube (the Booth Turf War Edition) – Sharpie squeaks on vinyl, shop hum, Styrofoam cup squeals, rookie wrench clatters, and sarcastic play-by-play from the peanut gallery.
Transcript

Title

Hope: Some folks pass the time with crosswords, church bulletins, or whittlin’ on porch rails. John and Fred? They spend it with burnt coffee, grease stains, and wagers stranger than a squirrel funeral. Today at the Jiffy Lube, Curtis drops in with sass, Lyle collects a brand-new nickname, and our boys critique the ballet of the maintenance bay.

[Harmonica wipe]

[Jiffy Door]

John: First rule of survival, Farts. … Always follow the smell of burnt coffee beans.

Fred: Second rule of survival, Dusty. … Ignore the sign that says ‘Employees Only.

John: And the third rule? … Make sure no man lays claim to your booth territory.

[John and Fred walk over to booth]

[sharpie sounds]

John: Curtis! You marking our turf like some kind of booth bandit?

Fred: Yeah, son — this is sacred ground. You don’t just roll up and tag the Farts’ territory.

Curtis: Oh … Hi, squirrel farts. Don’t worry. If you sit in front of it, nobody will see it…. My blind friend Jimmy will be over later to draw in stick figures and add phone numbers.

John: Curtis … I’m starting to look forward to your presence about as much as Bob’s burned bacon.

Intro

Fred: Ignore him, Curtis. He thinks transmission fluid is dark roast. … So, what brings you down to the Jiffy Joint today? … You down here getting your oil and filter changed?

Curtis: No, weirdo, but that’s exactly what a dipstick would ask. … I’m actually down here to get some advise from Lyle about my back end, and Lyle knows all about big back ends from hanging around you two.

[Lyle walks up]

Lyle: Well, lookie here. Hey everybody, it’s the pride of the class of 2001. Did you guys know that Curtis was voted …Most Likely to Need a Hall Pass at His Own 40th Birthday.

Curtis: Nice one. … Lyle! How’s your sister? … You know we went to Maple Grove High together. … Is she still trying to cheat on her math test using a thesaurus?

Lyle: No, but as a linguistics professor 20 years later, she does know how to say “loser” in 12 dialects. Now, What brings you down to the old oil exchange. stopped

[Lyle starts pouring coffee]

Curtis: Well, the back end on my Firebird tends to get a little loose in the downhill corner of Billy Walton’s dirt run. I thought it was marbles in the corners, but I think it’s a traction issue in the rear suspension.

Lyle: Oh, that’s an easy fix. Just look under the rear axle and find that stabilizer bar bracket. Give it a good turn, and after the bushing clamp clicks into place, it should grip them corners a lot tighter.

Fred: Wow, Curtis. You gonna be able to handle that all by yourself?

Curtis: Of course. I have 3 YouTube links and I pay for GPT Plus. … You so dumb! …

Fred: I dunno Curtis. They say wisdom comes with age.

Curtis: So does hair in weird places. … And speakin’ of weird places, my friend Jimmy was sitting in a recliner and threw out his back when he sneezed. … I gotta go take him to the chiropractor.

[Curtis runs off]

[door opens and shuts]

John: Militant squirrels. … Cake crumbs Curtis. … Maple Grove’s becomin’ infested with vermin.

Fred: Aww, come now, Dusty. Don’t get all twitchy. … You sound like a man who just lost a staring contest with a chipmunk.

Lyle: Yeah, relax John. … Don’t get your fluffy tail in a bunch. … Here, I’ll go grab you guys some fresh brew and some rubbing alcohol to clean off Curtis scrawling’s.

[Lyle walks off]

Fred: …You know, I don’t know if you caught this, Dusty, but on the drive over, I set the thermostat all the way north of seventy-three this morning.

John: Sure, Farts, I noticed, and I kindly thank ya. … And I don’t know if you noticed, but I wore an extra sweater today so I could crack the window. … Figured you might like a little breeze to keep from pitting up your shirt.

Fred: I noticed. And it was much appreciated. … And I sure am glad we got ‘that,’ settled.

Hope: That’s it? … You’re kidding, right? … The whole saga of settling things like temperature tantrum throwing goat herders in the parking lot? … and you wrap it all up with a polite nod to 73 degrees? … No slammed doors? No circles under your eyes for days? Not even an extra dish towel thrown in disgust? You. Gumpy. Old. Coots!

John: Knock it off, Hope. This is how old men get things done.

Fred: Yeah. We ain’t got time for grudges. C.A.F.E. ratings? yes. … Grudges, no.

John: Betting on Postal Carriers

John: Speaking of time. … You got today’s time tables, Farts? … I wanna see the odds before the first postmark.

Mending Fences

Fred: Yep. Fresh off the printer. Looks like ‘Express Lane Elaine’ is leadin’ the pack again. … Her route is tighter than a pickle jar at a church picnic.

John: Bah. I’m puttin’ my money on ‘Parcel Pete.’ … Man handles a cul-de-sac like Secretariat on derby day.

Fred: Suit yourself. But don’t underestimate ‘Overtime Otis.’ … He’s got endurance. Rain, snow, sleet, bad knees—don’t matter. … Otis still limps across that finish line.

[SFX: Footsteps. Lyle approaches with a fresh pot of coffee.]

Lyle: …What in the world are you two jabberin’ about? Sounds like the world’s saddest horse track.

John: Postal carriers, Lyle. … We chart their routes. We track their on-time deliveries. We know how many curbs they hop. … We wager on ‘em like thoroughbreds.

Fred: Got the whole timetable mapped. Every corner, every cul-de-sac, every motion sensor rigged to front hedges.

Lyle: …You’re kiddin’ me. You’re actually bettin’ on mailmen?

John: Postal stallions of suburbia, Lyle. … Show some respect.

Fred: And don’t knock it ‘til you’ve seen ‘Return-to-Sender Randy’ hit that round-a-bout during carpool hour over by the elementary school…. Pure poetry.

[Beat. John scribbles on a slip.]

John: Alright, Farts. Five on Pete finishing Elm Avenue before 3:10.

Fred: You’re on. And I’ll raise you three on Elaine hitting the stoplight before it cycles twice.

[SFX: Papers shuffle. Lyle snatches one up.]

Lyle: Wait—what? Five? Three? You two bettin’ real money on this nonsense?

John: Money? No, no. We ain’t fools.

Fred: Those are I.O.U.’s. for our Neighborly niceties. I win. he takes out Mildred’s trash for the next 5 days…. He wins. I mow Earl’s patchy yard for 3 months. … Keeps the stakes high but the wallets intact.

Lyle: …So you’re runnin’ an imaginary horse track made out of unknowing postal carriers, powered entirely by kindly neighborhood “Honey can do” lists?

John: Exactly. The only gambling operation in town where the house always wins—and by house, we mean the whole dang neighborhood.

Fred: Now hush, Pit Baby. Elaine’s rounding third, and Pearlie’s bushes ain’t trimming themselves.

Lyle: Well, gents… good service like that deserves good service in return. How about a little more sludge?

[SFX: Coffee pours.]

Fred: You’re a good man, Charlie Brown. … Now, if only this so-called smart watch could help me figure my winnings.

John: Oh? Is that the smart watch Aria talked you into?

Fred: Sure is. But between you and me, I don’t think it’s all that smart.

Lyle: Really? Looks brand new. Hold on—you still got the protective tape on it. State of the art! Shiny as the top of John’s head. I bet it’s smarter than all three of us put together.

Fred: Pfft. Smart? I think not. Thing kept tellin’ me to stand up when I was sittin’ in a perfectly good chair.

John: Maybe it just knows your backside better than you do.

Fred: Only reason I got it was ‘cause Aria guilted me. Said if my heart stopped, maybe it’d notify someone… eventually.

Lyle: Well, it’s got safety features for that. Emergency calls, fall detection… could come in handy.

Fred: Oh, I get all that. But tarnation if I can understand it. Here, Lyle—you take it. Your paw took a tumble recently. You’ll know how to put it to better use.

Lyle: Dang, Farts! Every time you come in here, you give me something. And these last two have been a lot better than those back rub coupons. You sure, though? What about your ticker?

Fred: Bah, that little scare was nothin’. Just three straight days of Bob’s chili. False alarm.

John: Besides, I’m with him nearly every wakin’ second nowadays.

Jiffy Gymnastics

Fred: He does keep an eye on me… just one though. Other one ain’t worth a truck o’ duck luck.

Lyle: Well, John. You keep on doing that then.

[Lyle walks off]

Hope: Every now and then, between the thermostat wars, the squirrel paranoia, and the Sharpie turf battles… these two stumble into something decent. … Fred, grumbling all the way, handed over his so-called smart watch not out of pride, but out of care. In their world, generosity comes wrapped in sarcasm, but it’s still generosity. … The watch found a smarter wrist, and the gesture—believe it or not—was heartfelt. But like everything at Jiffy Lube, it was also… not particularly sanitary. Ballet of the Maintenance Bay

Fred: Hold up. Hold up now. Look down in Bay Two… We got a rookie settin’ up for the oil filter twist.

John: Oh boy. That move separates the interns from the insurance claims.

Fred: I would say this is a bold, but Classic maneuver. … High risk, medium reward. … If he botches it, they’re gonna be moppin’ up 10W-30 and pride.

John: Cripes! … Look at that elbow placement—he’s leanin’ too far left.

Fred: I would say that there is a classic case of over-compensation. … He’s Probably watched too many YouTube tutorials. Sure don’t got that fluid feel.

John: You gotta finesse the filter! … Not strangle it! … Poor kid’s wrenchin’ like he’s openin’ a stuck pickle jar at a family reunion.

[metal clang]

John: And now, he’s lost his torque wrenches! This could cost him points with the Eastern Bloc judges.

Fred: Oooh, that’s a double-axle drain plug pirouette if I ever saw one.

Hope: Picture it: a rookie mechanic fumbles a filter, two old men in the booth provide play-by-play like it’s the World Gymnastics Championships, and not a soul in the garage asked for this coverage. … Yet here they are—rating elbow placement, torque angles, and pickle-jar technique as if the fate of the free world depended on it. … John and Fred once even tried to submit their ratings for Jiffy Lube mechanics to Car & Driver magazine. Their last envelope was returned, unopened. … With the words ‘Please Stop’ scrawled on the outside.

[Lyle walks back up]

Lyle: Hey fellas. Don’t tell the greasey boys. … But they really like you both judging their performances. They say it makes them laugh and keeps them o their toes. Just don’t tell them I told ya.

John: Fair enough, just see if they’ll help with the vermin problem.

[John and Fred stand]

Lyle: Will do. … And hey, thanks again for the watch. … Really appreciate it, even if it was quite random.

Gigi: Random? Hey Kids, that’s today’s “R” Word for the Day! … Can you say ‘Random’?

Fred: Random R.A.N.D.U.M. Ran. Dumb. I once banged my head. … And ran dumb for a while. Ran. Dumb.

[R word close]

[John and Fred walk out]

Hope: Talk about random. Today John and Fred wagered on United States Postal Carriers like they were thoroughbreds, judged rookie grease boys with Olympic-style commentary no one requested, and came to a peaceful cease fire in the endless war over the thermostat. And yet, in the middle of all the nonsense, they handed off a smart watch to someone who truly needed it. That’s not random at all. That’s intentional. Because in the end, the measure of a life isn’t the sharpness of your sarcasm. … The true measure … comes from the kindness of your character.
