Meet Maple Grove

The locals, catalogued for your protection.

The Locals

  • John “Dusty”

    The self-appointed brains of the operation — suspicious of squirrels, suspicious of thermostats, suspicious of anyone who disagrees with him about the one correct temperature for a diner (74°F, “the gentleman’s temperature”). Somewhere around Episode 14 he develops a detective alter ego he calls “Clue-dini,” which nobody encouraged and everybody now has to live with. (First spotted in Episode 1.)

  • Fred “Farts”

    The heart of the two, if you squint. Sentimental about coupons, cookies, and inventing rating systems for things that do not need rating systems (nuts, condiments). Complains constantly, means none of it, and has been known to prove it by giving away his own smart watch. (First spotted in Episode 1.)

  • Hope

    I narrate. That’s the job — conspiratorial commentary, half-wisdom, and a running record of grievances, mostly about two men and a booth. I did not choose this life. I don’t regret it, either, though you didn’t hear me say that. (First spotted in Episode 1.)

  • Aria

    Waitress and den mother of the Polyester Lounge, armed with sass, coffee, and zero tolerance for nonsense. Has since been drafted as referee for a goat-boxing match, Pumpkin-Spice Fairy on Halloween, and Power Compliance Officer on New Year’s Eve. None of this was in her job description. (First spotted in Episode 1.)

  • Doctor Bobby

    Runs the mobile blood-donation bus with the enthusiasm of a man trained somewhere the line between hospital and farm was blurry. Tells stories that start with a medical procedure and end with a goat. Wears a cursed calculator watch that has never once told the correct time. (First spotted in Episode 5.)

  • Doctor Fritz “Freudy”

    A therapist who has read exactly enough Freud to be dangerous. Mistakes sarcasm for progress, doodles for diagnosis, and once ran a therapy booth in a shopping mall food court until the health department had opinions about it. (First spotted in Episode 9.)

  • Curtis

    A man in his 40s who still lives with his mother and runs the town’s conspiracy-theory newsletter. Announces himself loudly, tags booths with Sharpie graffiti, and travels with Nut-Zilla, a homemade contraption of uncertain purpose and confirmed menace. (First spotted in Episode 8.)

  • Lyle

    Keeper of the Jiffy Lube coffee pot and dispenser of reality checks, delivered flat, dry, and entirely without enthusiasm. Not impressed. Never has been. (First spotted in Episode 2.)

  • Pearlie Fae

    Gravel-voiced frontwoman of The Rusty Hinges, the house band responsible for every Dusty Farts jingle. Announces her retirement approximately once a week and shows up to the next gig anyway. (First spotted in Episode 10.)

  • Suzy and Umie

    Arrived in a cloud of rhinestones and haven’t left. Sparkle engineers, vibe managers, and the reason Maple Grove’s New Year’s Eve ball carries more glitter than any small town’s power grid can safely support. (First spotted in Episode 16.)

The Landmarks

  • Polyester Lounge

    The boys’ home booth. Bottomless coffee, cracked vinyl, and a neon sign that’s the closest thing this show has to a permanent set.

  • Jiffy Lube

    Free coffee, a captive audience, and Lyle’s disapproval, all included with the oil change nobody’s actually getting.

  • Doctor Bobby’s Blood Bus

    A converted RV where donating blood comes with recliners, cookies, and more medical opinions than are strictly licensed.

  • Maple Gump Park

    Home to the Booth of Cherished Chaplain Clarence Booths, a park bench with more backstory than most people, and a squirrel population John considers organized labor.

  • Menards

    A hardware store that, for one memorable episode, became a two-toilet diner booth. Ask no further questions.

  • Maple Grove YMCA

    Gym, community hall, and — briefly, disastrously — group therapy space.
